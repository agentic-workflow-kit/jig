> altitude of jig ahead of the two Wave 1 story briefs: `w1-s1-configuration-and-work-domain` and

> collide with the M5a slice's own frame at
> [`../../../../design/notes/problem-frame.md`](../../../../design/notes/problem-frame.md), which framed
> a narrower runtime-execution slice at a different altitude and remains that slice's record.

> **Resolution status (coordinator, post-frame).** The three `requires approval` items in §3/§4 were
> resolved before authoring and are recorded in [`decisions.md`](./decisions.md): **D-001** — jig owns
> the type/shape/invariants of Policy/Work-profile/Repo-floors, the owner authors instances; **D-002** —

> one entity with two lifecycle phases (the user's decision). The §4 AgreedSystemModel below reflects
> these resolutions; the §3 InputResolution table is preserved as the as-framed intake snapshot.

## 1. Scope and Goal

- **Source request:** deep-design track, Wave 1 — frame jig's domain model at the altitude of
  entities, ubiquitous language, ownership, relations, seams, and lifecycle _terms_ (not state
  machines) for two domain areas: **Configuration & Work** (Track, Execution plan, Work item,
  Policy, Repo-level floors, Work profile) and **Runtime & Observation** (Run, Evidence, Notice,
  the run-records / event-log evidence model).
- **Goal:** produce an `AgreedSystemModel` clean and citable enough to seed the Wave 1 charter and
  both story briefs, and to be read by later waves' frames without rework. The model must name
  entity responsibilities (owns / reads / does-not-own), relations, seams, and lifecycle
  vocabulary — continuing the existing invariant and context vocabulary (INV-001..008,
  CTX-001..005, CFG-1..10, GUARD-1, SEE-1..6, MERGE-1/3/4) rather than resetting it.
- **Out of scope for this wave:** state machines and transition tables for Run and Work item
  (Wave 2); the Fence, Doorbell, and Orchestration **behavioral** contexts as anything more than
  named adjacent contexts the relations point to; field-level schema or a frozen contract for the
  execution plan or the observability records (both remain v0, unfrozen); package/module layout;
  any code, TypeScript interfaces, or JSON Schema; enforcement rules; delivery sequencing beyond
  what the charter needs as a pointer.

### Blocking Questions

None. Every open item above is resolvable by the coordinator/owner choosing between named, sourced alternatives (`requires approval`); none requires new information the owner alone can supply beyond a choice among documented options, so nothing rises to `blocked`.

### Safe Assumptions

- Orchestration, Fence, and Doorbell are named as adjacent behavioral contexts only, not modeled in entity depth this wave (Wave 2's territory). Risk: low, explicit in the coordinator's brief.
- Evidence is a conceptual/vocabulary entity backed entirely by the Records log, no separate store. Risk: low, corroborated by two independent sources (`core/README.md`, `records.md`).
- Notice is a named projection of the Records log, no separate store. Risk: low, corroborated by `records.md`'s diagram.
- Repo-level floors is a distinct entity related to Policy by a one-directional "tightens, never weakens" relation. Risk: low.
- Track is the aggregation root for the per-track configuration group, related to Execution plan / Policy / Work profile by reference (1:1 current-version each), not embedding. Risk: low.
- Work profile carries no gating authority; it is always downstream of Policy. Risk: low.

### Seams and External Boundaries

- **Execution-plan contract v0** — the versioned seam between whatever produced the plan (upstream, optional per `jig.md`) and jig; Plan intake validates against it; not frozen.
- **Observability-records contract v0** — the versioned seam between jig's Run records and any downstream consumer (owner inspection, future Learning loop); not frozen.
- **Plan intake boundary** — the point where an owner-authored plan instance becomes a `ValidatedPlan`; reject-unknown-format is enforced here, once, at the boundary (no downstream re-validation).
- **Policy/Work-profile reference boundary** — the plan references Policy and Work profile by identity/version rather than embedding content; this is the seam that keeps a run from silently weakening its own guardrails (GUARD-1).
- **Repo-floor inheritance boundary** — the one-directional constraint from Repo-level floors down into every Track's Policy; changing the floor is itself a governed action, per `concepts.md`.
- The four driver seams (Agent, Execution Host, Forge, Work Source) are out of this wave's entity scope; they are named here only as the boundary Execution plan's "Stack-Seam Requirements" section references, and remain Wave 2 / contracts-track territory.

### Lifecycle and State Terms

Per the coordinator's scope, this wave records lifecycle _terms_ only — not the transition tables, which are Wave 2's (`orchestration.md` owns the closed transition table).

- **Work item terms** (product/design, unchanged from `concepts.md` and `orchestration.md`): `eligible → started → parked (transient) → done | landed | rejected | blocked`. `done` and `landed` are distinct milestones (MERGE-4); `parked` is transient and resolves to resume or `rejected`.
- **Run terms**: `previewed → started → stopped | resumed | completed`. `stopped` is run-level, not a work-item outcome.
- **Policy term**: "fixed at launch" (GUARD-1) — Policy does not have a lifecycle of its own at this altitude; it is bound once per run and held immutable for that run's duration.
- **Plan terms**: a plan instance is either accepted (`ValidatedPlan`) or rejected (with a named reason); this is a boundary decision, not a multi-state lifecycle.

## 7. Complexity Drivers

- **Invariants:** GUARD-1 (policy fixed at launch); CFG-1/2/3 (policy vs. work-profile split, per-track, repo floors as inherited, non-weakenable constraint); CFG-4 (derived, not hand-set — live behavior is computed from Policy + Plan, not stored directly); MERGE-1/3/4 (evidence gates landing; done-conditions explicit and policy-bound; done and landed are separate milestones); SEE-3 (records are the evidence, no parallel narrative); INV-006 (state/summary/notices are pure projections, never hand-authored); INV-007 (reject unknown plan formats, no silent guessing).
- **State transitions:** named at term level only this wave (work item: eligible→started→parked→done/landed/rejected/blocked; run: previewed→started→stopped/resumed/completed); the transition tables themselves are Wave 2.
- **Integrations / anti-corruption:** the four driver seams are referenced by the plan's "Stack-Seam Requirements" but are out of this wave's entity scope; the plan-intake and records contracts are the two versioned seams this wave's entities sit directly against.
- **Consistency / idempotency / replay / audit:** the Records log is append-only, single-leased-writer, and the sole source for projections, notices, and export (SEE-1..6); this wave must keep Evidence and Notice from acquiring a second, competing notion of "current state."
- **Security / authorization:** out of entity scope this wave (Fence/Doorbell are Wave 2); this wave's obligation is only to not misplace Policy's gating authority onto Work profile (CFG-2) or onto Configuration & Work more broadly.
- **Migration / deploy:** none — this is a docs-only frame; no schema freeze, no package layout.
- **Observability:** central to Runtime & Observation — Run records must remain the single source Evidence and Notice project from, per SEE-1..6 and `records.md`.
- **Testing:** none at this altitude — this wave produces no code; the eventual story briefs will carry test/validation expectations forward per the ddd profile's required artifacts.

## 8. Architecture Mode and Initial DDD Depth

**Selected architecture_mode:** `system-entity-model`

**Why this mode fits:** Wave 1's deliverable is an entity → responsibility → ownership map across two domain areas, consistent with and refining the existing spine map in `design/core/README.md`. DDD's strategic tools (bounded contexts, ubiquitous language, ownership) make that map's boundaries and vocabulary explicit; they are the lens applied on top of the entity model, not the first cut. `strategic-ddd` was considered and rejected as the primary label because the wave's actual output — an entity/responsibility/ownership table plus relations and seams — is the system-entity-model artifact shape; the strategic-DDD concerns (bounded contexts, ubiquitous language) are captured here as a DDD projection of that model (§6), consistent with the `frame-technical-design` skill's Step 5 guidance ("context candidates as a DDD projection of the system model, not... the first architecture shape").

**Selected depth:** `strategic-only`

**Why this depth fits:** the brief is explicit that this wave frames "the domain-model altitude," names "entity responsibilities... relations, seams, and the lifecycle _terms_ (not the state machines — those are Wave 2)." No aggregates, value objects, commands, or domain events are being authored this wave; ownership and language are the entire ask. This wave does **not** inherit M5a's `use-case-slices` depth: that depth was selected for a narrower runtime-execution slice with concrete commands/use-cases (ValidatePlan, StartDryRun, AuthorizeRequest, etc.) at a lower altitude than this wave's domain-model frame. Naming the four driver seams as boundaries (§Seams) does not raise this wave's depth to `ports-and-adapters` — a seam is named as an external boundary the model touches, not designed at this wave.

**Where tactical depth is intentionally omitted:** everywhere. No entity in this wave gets aggregate/value-object/domain-event treatment; that escalation, if ever needed, is a later-wave decision made deliberately (consistent with the M5a design's own precedent of recording "why not" rather than defaulting to it).
